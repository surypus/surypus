Self Care for Open Source Maintainers

Maintaining an open source project is rewarding, but it can also be demanding. This guide offers practical advice for taking care of yourself while doing meaningful work.

Why Self Care Matters

Open source maintainership can be rewarding, but it also comes with unique challenges. Maintainers often face:

  • Constant pressure to respond to issues and pull requests
  • Unrealistic expectations from users and sponsors
  • Isolation and loneliness
  • Time management challenges
  • Burnout from overcommitting

Your well-being as a maintainer is essential. When you're healthy and balanced, you can better serve your community.

Understanding Your Motivation

Why do you do open source work? Knowing your "why" helps you stay motivated and make better decisions.

Exercise: Write down your top 5 motivations for maintaining Surypus.

Common motivations include:

  • Helping others solve problems
  • Learning new skills
  • Building reputation and career
  • Working on something you care about
  • Making a positive impact on the world
  • Connecting with a community

Your motivations will evolve over time. Revisit this list periodically.

Recognizing Burnout Signs

Burnout is common among open source maintainers. Watch for these warning signs:

Category Signs
Emotional Feeling drained, cynical, or detached
Physical Exhaustion, headaches, sleep problems
Cognitive Can't focus, forget details, reduced productivity
Behavioral Avoiding work, procrastinating, withdrawing
Relational Irritable, impatient, less empathetic

Self-Assessment:

Rate these statements on a scale of 1-5 (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree):

  1. I feel exhausted at the thought of working on the project. [___]
  2. I've become more cynical about the project or users. [___]
  3. I've lost interest in work I used to enjoy. [___]
  4. I feel like nothing I do makes a difference. [___]
  5. I'm avoiding the project or putting it off. [___]
  6. My sleep or health has been affected. [___]
  7. I feel overwhelmed by the workload. [___]

Score: ____ / 35

  • 14-21: Some burnout risk. Take action soon.
  • 22-28: Moderate burnout. Make significant changes.
  • 29-35: High burnout risk. Consider stepping back.

Strategies for Sustaining Your Energy

Set Boundaries

What boundaries do you need?

Write down your boundaries:

  • Time: I work on Surypus on [days] from [time] to [time].
  • Scope: I will not work on [types of contributions] because...
  • Communication: I respond to issues within [timeframe].
  • Availability: I am unavailable on [dates/days].

Exercise: Draft your boundary statement.

"I am available to work on Surypus on weekdays from 9am to 5pm EST. I will respond to issues within 48 hours. I am taking a break from reviewing PRs on weekends."

Why boundaries matter:

  • They protect your time and energy
  • They set clear expectations for others
  • They help you avoid overcommitting
  • They model healthy behavior for the community

Remember: Saying "no" is okay. You don't owe anyone your time.

Take Breaks

Schedule regular breaks:

  • Daily breaks (e.g., 15 minutes every 2 hours)
  • Weekly breaks (e.g., no open source on weekends)
  • Vacation breaks (e.g., one week off per quarter)
  • Sabbatical breaks (e.g., one month off per year)

How to take a break effectively:

  1. Plan ahead - Tell your community in advance
  2. Update your status - Set your GitHub status and auto-responder
  3. Delegate tasks - Assign someone to handle urgent matters
  4. Disconnect - Actually step away from the project
  5. Return gradually - Start with small tasks when you come back

Protect your sleep, exercise, and social connections. These are foundational to your well-being.

Build a Support Network

Who can you lean on?

  • Other maintainers - Share experiences and advice
  • Friends and family - Talk about your fears and frustrations
  • Community members - Appreciate their support and feedback
  • Professional help - Consider therapy or counseling if needed

Join communities like:

  • Maintainer Community
  • Open Source Mental Health
  • Local meetups and user groups

Celebrate Successes

It's easy to focus on what's broken. Make time to recognize what's working.

Celebrate:

  • 🎉 First-time contributors
  • ⭐ New milestones (stars, downloads, adopters)
  • 🐛 Bugs fixed
  • 📖 Documentation improved
  • 🤝 Community connections made

Keep a victory log. Write down 3 things you're proud of each week.

Practice Self-Compassion

Maintaining open source is hard. Be kind to yourself.

  • Forgive yourself for mistakes and imperfections
  • Accept your limits - You can't do everything
  • Focus on impact - What you do matters, even if it's not perfect
  • Ask for help - It's okay to need support

Check-In Questions

Ask yourself regularly:

  1. Am I enjoying my work on this project?
  2. Am I energized or drained after working on it?
  3. Am I meeting my own needs (sleep, health, relationships)?
  4. Am I setting and keeping boundaries?
  5. Do I have support when I need it?

If you're struggling, reach out. You're not alone.
