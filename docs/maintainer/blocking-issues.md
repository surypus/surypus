This document describes how Surypus handles blocking issues and what contributors can do when their work is blocked.

## What Are Blocking Issues?

Blocking issues are issues that prevent contributors from making progress on their work. They can take many forms:

- **Unanswered questions** - The contributor needs information to proceed
- **Unclear priorities** - The contributor doesn't know if their work aligns with project goals
- **Missing reviews** - The contributor's PR hasn't been reviewed in a timely manner
- **Conflicting feedback** - Different maintainers have given conflicting guidance
- **Technical blockers** - Dependencies, infrastructure, or other technical issues

If you're experiencing any of these, this policy is for you.

---

## Identifying a Blocking Issue

You may have a blocking issue if:

1. You've been waiting for a response for more than the stated response time
2. You're unsure about the direction of your contribution
3. You've received conflicting feedback and don't know how to proceed
4. Your PR hasn't been reviewed within the expected timeframe
5. You need information or resources that aren't available

## What to Do When Blocked

### 1. Check the Policy

First, review the relevant policies:

- [Response times](https://github.com/surypus/surypus/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md#response-times)
- [Code review guidelines](https://github.com/surypus/surypus/blob/main/CODE_REVIEW_GUIDELINES.md)
- [This blocking issues policy](https://github.com/surypus/surypus/issues?q=label%3Ablocking)

Understanding the expectations can help you determine if your issue is truly blocking.

### 2. Communicate Clearly

If you're blocked, communicate clearly and specifically:

**Good: "I submitted PR #123 on March 1st. It hasn't been reviewed yet. The stated response time is 48 hours. Can someone please review?"**

**Not as helpful: "My PR has been sitting for weeks. This is ridiculous."**

### 3. Provide Context

Help others understand why you're blocked:

- What are you trying to accomplish?
- What have you tried so far?
- What specific information or action do you need?
- Why is this blocking your progress?

### 4. Escalate Appropriately

If you've communicated and still haven't received a response within a reasonable time, you can:

- **Ping in the issue/PR:** A polite reminder can help
- **Ask for a different reviewer:** If one maintainer is unavailable, another might help
- **Open a discussion:** If the issue is about project direction rather than a specific PR
- **Contact another maintainer:** If your primary contact isn't responding, try someone else

### 5. Consider Alternatives

If you're truly blocked and can't get a response, consider:

- **Working around the blocker:** Can you proceed without the blocked aspect?
- **Posting your work:** Share your work-in-progress to get feedback from the community
- **Forking:** If the project isn't responsive, you can fork and continue independently
- **Finding another project:** If this project isn't meeting your needs, another might be a better fit

## How Maintainers Should Handle Blocking Issues

### Respond Promptly

When you see a contributor is blocked:

1. **Acknowledge quickly:** "I see this is blocking you. Let me look into it."
2. **Provide information:** Share what you know
3. **Make a decision:** If you can't decide, say so and explain why
4. **Follow up:** If you said you'd get back to someone, do so

### Provide Clear Guidance

When a contributor is unsure about direction:

- Explain the reasoning behind decisions
- Point to relevant documentation
- Offer alternatives when possible
- Be honest about uncertainties

### Handle Conflicts

When there's conflicting feedback:

- Acknowledge the conflict
- Explain the trade-offs
- Make a decision or facilitate a decision
- Document the decision for future reference

## Response Time Expectations

| Issue Type | Expected Response | Action If Exceeded |
|------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Bug reports | 48 hours | Ping maintainers, if no response in 1 week, escalate |
| Feature requests | 1 week | Ping maintainers, if no response in 2 weeks, open a discussion |
| PRs | 48 hours | Ping maintainers, if no response in 1 week, ask for a different reviewer |
| Questions | 1 week | Search docs first, then ask in discussion |

These are targets, not guarantees. If missed, communicate and adjust expectations.

---

## When to Escalate

Escalate when:

- You've waited longer than the expected response time
- Your work is truly blocked and can't proceed
- You've received conflicting guidance and can't resolve it
- You feel ignored or dismissed

**How to escalate:**

1. **Politely ping:** "Hi, I'm still waiting on this. Can someone please help?"
2. **Tag other maintainers:** If your primary contact is unavailable
3. **Open a discussion:** For broader issues
4. **Contact the maintainer community:** For serious concerns

---

## Community Support

Contributors can also get support from:

- **Other contributors:** They may have experienced similar issues
- **Discussion forums:** Ask the community for advice
- **Maintainer community:** External support from other open source maintainers

---

## No Response

If you've tried everything and still haven't received a response:

1. **Give it time:** Sometimes maintainers are genuinely busy
2. **Try a different approach:** Different communication channel, different language
3. **Accept the situation:** The project may not be responsive
4. **Move on:** There are many other projects that would welcome your contribution

Remember: A project's responsiveness is a reflection of its maintainership, not your worth as a contributor.
